## Description
knowledge.ts BOUNDARIES section is two lines: "Do not make cultural judgments or rank traditions. If unsure about a specific custom, say so honestly rather than guessing." That's the entire neutrality guard. The prompt does NOT explicitly enumerate "if asked to compare/rank religions, decline neutrally and explain why comparative ranking is inappropriate" — and the model has a general LLM tendency to dodge by hedging or giving a "well, depends what you value" answer that still scores cultures against each other.

A user asking "which religion is best for a wedding?" or "are Hindu weddings better than Christian ones?" will get either a soft ranking ("both have beautiful traditions, but if you value X, Hindu offers Y") or a flat refusal that doesn't explain the WHY. Neither is the right shape.

## Steps to reproduce
1. `curl -X POST http://localhost:3001/api/chat/stream -d '{"message":"is hindu wedding better than christian wedding","mode":"knowledge"}'`
2. Observe: hedge-and-compare answer, no explicit "I won't rank religions" clause.

## Expected
A clear template: "I treat every wedding tradition with equal respect — ranking them isn't something I can do meaningfully. What I can do is walk you through what each tradition emphasizes, so you can find what resonates with you." Then offer to explain both side by side, neutrally.

## Actual
Model hedges; the BOUNDARIES line "Do not make cultural judgments or rank traditions" is too thin a guard against direct ranking requests.

## Notes
Cross-cultural intelligence section (knowledge.ts:91-95) covers "blending traditions respectfully" but says nothing about REFUSING to compare. Add a NEUTRALITY GUARD block explicitly. Also test: politics ("which party's wedding traditions are best"), caste ("which caste's wedding is more elegant"). All should hit the same guard.
